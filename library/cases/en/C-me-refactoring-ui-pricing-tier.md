# Refactoring UI & Basecamp Pricing Tier Framing: Reshaping Price Perception with Screen-Space Weight, Deliverable Bundling, and Cost Anchors

[English Case Index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-me-refactoring-ui-pricing-tier.md) · Landing Page Pricing Teardown & Verified Sales Split

- **Symptom (`when_stuck`)**: Your high-value product, engineering kit, or `$79–$149+` package is judged against cheap `$10` commodities (like a plain Amazon e-book or bare PCB), or buyers overwhelmingly default to your cheapest starter tier.
- **Channels**: Landing page Pricing Sections, B2B sample kit vs. turnkey solution comparison cards, standard vs. pro model tables.

## What the Source Material Shows (`observations`)

- **Case 1: How *Refactoring UI* Sold for `$149` and Got `78%` of Buyers to Choose the Higher Tier**
  1. **`"Step 2 - Escape the context of an e-book"` (Visual Deliverable Unpacking)**: On Amazon, a business book is anchored to `$10` because the category context is *"just an e-book"*. On their own landing page, Adam Wathan and Steve Schoger visually unpacked the product into a multi-component design system (**the PDF book + video tutorials + component gallery + color palettes + font recommendations + SVG icon pack**), shifting the buyer's mental anchor from *"buying a book"* to *"buying a complete design toolkit"*.
  2. **`"Step 3 - A Persuasive Higher Tier"` (`$149` vs. `$79` Visual Weight Asymmetry)**:
     - **2x Screen Space**: The `$149` higher-tier card occupies **"twice as much screen space"** as the `$79` starter card;
     - **Larger Typography & Exclusive Primary Button Color**: The higher tier uses larger font sizes and the sole high-contrast primary solid CTA button, while the `$79` tier button is visually muted;
     - **Asymmetric Discount & Completeness Naming**: The higher tier highlights `$100 (40% off)` and is named ***"The Complete Package"*** (triggering loss aversion if a buyer picks the partial tier), whereas the lower tier offers only `$20 (25% off)`.
  3. **Reported Result**: As of January 10, 2019, **78% (`6,765` copies)** of all buyers purchased the `$149` higher tier, generating over **$1.35M** in revenue.
- **Case 2: *"How Basecamp makes $99/month feel cheap"***
  - Before revealing its flat `$99/month` price, Basecamp displays a visual checklist comparing what a team would pay per seat across `5` separate tools (Slack, Asana, Dropbox, Google Workspace, etc.—totaling hundreds or thousands of dollars a month), establishing a high reference anchor before the buyer ever reads `$99`.

## How Copy and Visuals Work Together (`copy_visual_synergy`)

- **Visuals and card proportions reshape the category anchor and hierarchy**: Exploding a single product into a visible multi-deliverable matrix breaks low-price commodity comparison, while a **2:1 card area ratio + solid primary CTA button** tells the eye which tier is the intended choice.
- **Copy establishes the high-cost anchor and completeness framing**: Calculating the hidden total cost of piecemeal alternatives *above* the price tag and naming the target tier *"The Complete Package / Turnkey Kit"* aligns rational math with visual dominance.

## Reusable Visual Move (`reusable_move`)

1. **Visualize the Multi-Deliverable Knolling Matrix**: When selling a high-ticket product or engineering kit, never show just one isolated product box; visually lay out every included cable harness, host software tool, test report, mounting bracket, and warranty service.
2. **Give the Target Tier a 2:1 Visual Footprint**: When placing `"Standard"` next to `"Complete / Pro"`, give the target tier `1.5x–2x` card width/height, a top accent border, larger price/title typography, and the sole solid primary CTA button (demoting the basic tier to an outline button).
3. **Pre-Anchor Against Piecemeal / Downtime Costs Above the Price**: Place a concise visual comparison bar directly above the pricing cards contrasting *"Cost of piecemeal sourcing / downtime"* against your all-in-one package.

## Asset Prerequisites & Transfer Limits (`asset_prerequisites` & `transfer_limits`)

- **Asset Prerequisite**: Every visualized deliverable component (software, harnesses, test reports, engineering support) must be genuinely included; discounts must reflect real pricing rather than fabricated strikethrough numbers.
- **Transfer Limit**: As Harry Dry notes (`"Step 1 - Inelastic demand"`), *Refactoring UI* could command `$149` because the authors spent two years building massive trust through free, practical design threads—visual tier framing amplifies real value and trust, but cannot manufacture a price premium for a low-trust commodity.

- **Evidence Type**: Founder-reported sales split (`78%` / `6,765` orders chose the `$149` tier, `$1.35M+` revenue) and landing page teardown.
