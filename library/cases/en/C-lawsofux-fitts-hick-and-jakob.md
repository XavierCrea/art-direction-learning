# Laws of UX Fitts's Law, Hick's Law & Jakob's Law: Mobile Thumb Targets, Progressive Disclosure & Native Platform Conventions

[English Case Index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-lawsofux-fitts-hick-and-jakob.md) · Cognitive Psychology & Modern Mobile UI / Social Safe Zones

- **Symptom (`when_stuck`)**: Mobile primary CTAs are too small, trapped out of single-hand thumb reach, or packed so tightly that users mis-tap; too many SKUs/options displayed at once trigger choice paralysis; or custom non-standard UI patterns inside social/e-commerce ecosystems force users to relearn basic interactions.
- **Channels**: Mobile Landing Pages & H5, E-Commerce PDPs & SKU Selectors, 9:16 Social Video/Feed Overlays & UI Components.

## What the Source Material Shows (`observations`)

- **Fitts's Law**:
  - Formulated by psychologist **Paul Fitts (1954)**: *"The time to acquire a target is a function of the distance to and size of the target."*
  - Key takeaways on `lawsofux.com`: Touch targets must be large enough for accurate selection (standard mobile floor ≥ `44×44px` / `48×48dp`), separated by ample spacing to prevent mis-taps, and positioned in easily reachable areas (such as screen edges or mobile thumb-friendly zones).
- **Hick's Law & Choice Overload**:
  - Grounded in **William Edmund Hick & Ray Hyman (1952–1953)** and **Alvin Toffler (1970)**: *"The time it takes to make a decision increases with the number and complexity of choices."* Excessive choices cause choice overload and abandonment.
  - Key takeaways on `lawsofux.com`: Minimize options when response time is critical; break complex decisions into smaller steps; **highlight recommended options**; apply **progressive disclosure** to hide secondary/advanced controls until needed; and provide side-by-side comparison or filtering when multiple choices are unavoidable.
- **Jakob's Law**:
  - Coined by **Jakob Nielsen** (NN/g): *"Users spend most of their time on other sites. This means that users prefer your site to work the same way as all the other sites they already know."*
  - Key takeaways on `lawsofux.com`: Leverage established mental models and platform conventions so users focus on the product rather than learning a novel interface; provide smooth transitions when introducing major redesigns.

## How Copy and Visuals Work Together (`copy_visual_synergy`)

- **Spatial ergonomics & platform conventions remove interaction friction**: Oversized bottom-zone CTAs fall under the thumb, grouped/highlighted recommendations cut visual scanning time, and respecting native 9:16 Safe Zones keeps text clear of system overlays.
- **Short scenario tags do the first round of filtering for the buyer**: Labeling SKUs or tiers with concrete use-case microcopy (e.g., `"Best for 90% of Homes"`, `"Compact Travel"`, `"Industrial Heavy-Duty"`) collapses decision time immediately.

## Reusable Visual Move (`reusable_move`)

1. **Enforce Mobile Touch Targets & 9:16 Safe Zones (Fitts + Jakob)**:
   - Place the primary CTA inside the lower-middle thumb zone or a sticky bottom bar with touch dimensions ≥ `44×44px` and ≥ `8–12px` separation between adjacent controls;
   - For 9:16 vertical social creatives (TikTok / Douyin / Reels), keep headlines, price tags, and key visuals strictly clear of the platform's right-side engagement rail and bottom caption/CTA Safe Zones.
2. **Collapse Multi-Option Paralysis via Recommended Anchors & Progressive Disclosure (Hick + Choice Overload)**:
   - Whenever SKUs, plans, or filter chips exceed 3–4 items, stop displaying them with flat equal weight: group by scenario, visually highlight **1 recommended option**, and tuck advanced engineering specs behind an expandable drawer (`Progressive Disclosure`).
3. **Respect Native Platform Mental Models (Jakob)**:
   - Follow familiar icon semantics, price placement, and swipe gestures of the host ecosystem rather than inventing opaque controls for the sake of novelty.

## Asset Prerequisites & Transfer Limits (`asset_prerequisites` & `transfer_limits`)

- **Asset Prerequisite**: Highlighting a "Recommended" option must be grounded in genuine best-seller data or a clear use-case fit; 9:16 layouts require up-to-date Safe Zone overlays for the target platform.
- **Transfer Limit**: Never simplify away critical technical specs required for an informed B2B/high-ticket purchase—use semantic grouping, filters, and progressive disclosure instead of deleting necessary data.
