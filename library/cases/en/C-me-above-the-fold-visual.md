# Marketing Examples Above-the-Fold 5-Element Architecture: Replacing Abstract Illustrations with the "Product in Action"

[English Case Index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-me-above-the-fold-visual.md) · Landing Page Teardown & Conversion Structure Guide

- **Symptom (`when_stuck`)**: A landing page or storefront hero section (`Above the Fold`) shows generic flat illustrations, abstract 3D blobs, or stock handshake photos paired with vague slogans—leaving visitors unable to tell what physical product or tool you actually sell within 3 seconds, and hesitant to click the CTA button.
- **Channels**: Brand website Hero Sections, B2B storefront homepages, product launch landing pages.

## What the Source Material Shows (`observations`)

- In *My step-by-step guide to landing pages that convert*, Harry Dry breaks down high-converting landing pages into **5 Above-the-Fold Elements** and **5 Below-the-Fold Elements**:
  - **Part 1: Above the Fold (5 Elements)**:
    1. **Title**: Promises a specific outcome or calls out what makes you different;
    2. **Subtitle**: Sits directly below the title and explains specifically *how* the product delivers that claim;
    3. **Visual**: Harry Dry states the rule verbatim:
       > *"Don't show me fancy illustrations. Show me your product. Or even better, your product in action."*
    4. **Social Proof**: Places concrete numbers, customer logos, or testimonials right inside the first viewport to make the headline claim believable;
    5. **CTA (`Call to Action + Objection Handler`)**: Replaces cold labels like `Sign Up` or `Submit` with value-repeating copy, paired with a single line of microcopy directly underneath the button to handle the primary click objection upfront (e.g., `"No credit card required"`, `"45-second setup"`, or `"MOQ 1 Sample Available"`).
  - **Part 2: Below the Fold (5 Elements)**:
    6. **Features and Objections** (paired with real product visuals and specific numbers rather than fluffy adjectives);
    7. **More Social Proof** (action-inspiring customer results);
    8. **FAQ** (answering lingering buyer objections);
    9. **2nd CTA** (reminding the scrolled reader why they are here);
    10. **Founder's Note**: A personal sign-off with a real human face and signature at the bottom to build human-to-human trust.

## How Copy and Visuals Work Together (`copy_visual_synergy`)

- **The copy block (left/top) states the outcome and removes click friction**: The Title promises the result, the Subtitle explains the mechanism, and the CTA microcopy disarms the risk of clicking.
- **The visual block (right/bottom) delivers "seeing-is-believing operational proof"**: Showing the real physical product or live software interface actively performing its core job (`Product in Action`) lets the buyer's eyes verify the Subtitle's claim without mental guesswork.

## Reusable Visual Move (`reusable_move`)

1. **Kill Decorative Hero Illustrations & Generic Stock Photos**: Replace the right `45%–55%` of the desktop hero (or the area directly below the headline) with **the real product in operation** (e.g., a BMS board wired up with active cell-balancing telemetry on screen, rather than a glowing blue abstract globe).
2. **Lock Proximity Across the Above-the-Fold 5-Piece Cluster**: Group `[Title + Concrete Subtitle]` $\rightarrow$ `[High-Contrast Primary CTA + Under-Button Objection Microcopy]` $\rightarrow$ `[1-Line Verifiable Social Proof / Certification Bar]` into a tight visual unit.
3. **Alternate "One Feature / One Objection" Blocks Below the Fold**: Dedicate each subsequent section to proving one capability or disarming one purchasing objection, closing at the bottom with a signed Founder's / Chief Engineer's Note and a 2nd CTA.

## Asset Prerequisites & Transfer Limits (`asset_prerequisites` & `transfer_limits`)

- **Asset Prerequisite**: Requires authentic product photography, live operational shots, or real UI screenshots, plus truthful fulfillment terms for any under-button microcopy claims (lead time, MOQ, free trial).
- **Transfer Limit**: Never use unrelated abstract 3D geometry as a hero visual; keep only **1 primary solid CTA button** in the hero viewport so competing buttons do not split visual attention.
