# Coca-Cola "Recycle Me": Real-Object Symbol Deformation & 95% Monochrome Color-Field Minimalism

[English Case Index](../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-coca-cola-recycle-me-deformation.md) · Viral OOH & Print Craft Teardown

- **Symptom (`when_stuck`)**: Sustainability pledges, physical durability claims, or brand-attitude OOH posters easily turn preachy or visually cluttered—failing to stop passersby in noisy cityscapes when you want a 0.5-second, zero-lecture visual cue for a single physical action (crushing, folding, bending, or absorbing impact).
- **Channels**: High-Traffic Outdoor LED Billboards (OOH), Brand Attitude Print Posters, Minimalist Social Brand Cards.

## What the Source Material Shows (`observations`)

- **Context & Source**: **Coca-Cola** global recycling campaign *Recycle Me* (April 2024, documented in Famous Campaigns, *"Why Coca Cola have crushed their iconic logo"*).
- **Capture 1 (Urban Corner Mega LED Screen)**: An almost-square corner LED wall flooded with **≥95% pure Coca-Cola Red**, cutting cleanly through grey urban stone facades. Dead center sits a single crushed white Coca-Cola script logo; near the bottom sits a single tiny white uppercase verb — **`RECÍCLAME`** (*"Recycle Me"*). Zero can photography, zero recycling bin icon, zero explanatory copy.
- **Capture 2 (Print Poster Execution)**: A full-bleed solid Coca-Cola Red vertical poster with a crushed white wordmark centered on the canvas and **literally zero body or CTA copy**, letting the real crease lines and angular compression carry the entire message.
- **Physical Extraction Method**: As reported in the article, the distorted marks were not faked with generic warp filters—they were **extracted directly from real crushed Coca-Cola cans**, preserving authentic aluminum crease geometry and visual tension.

## How Copy and Visuals Work Together (`copy_visual_synergy`)

- **The deformed mark triggers a 0.5-second mental reconstruction**: Because viewers hold an ultra-sharp mental baseline of the intact Coca-Cola script, seeing it creased and compressed like a real aluminum can creates immediate visual dissonance that the brain resolves on its own (*"crushed can → recycle"*).
- **0–1 word copy avoids preaching**: Restricting text to a tiny single-word verb (`RECÍCLAME`)—or zero words in print—keeps 100% of the visual budget on the pure color field and real-world physical distortion.

## Reusable Visual Move (`reusable_move`)

1. **Replace Preachy Copy with Authentic Physical Deformation**:
   - When communicating a concrete physical action or material property (recycling compression, cable flexibility, crush resistance, foldability), skip lifestyle lectures and isolate **one unmistakably recognized symbol or physical object captured in authentic real-world mechanical stress** (scanned or photographed—never cheap digital warp filters).
2. **Flood `≥90%` of the Frame with One Pure Brand Color Field**:
   - Center the single high-contrast deformed graphic inside a `≥90%` solid brand color field (reinforcing `M-028` high-noise billboard denoising) with zero borders, badges, or background clutter.
3. **Cap Copy at `0–1` Action Words**:
   - Close with a tiny centered 1–3 word action verb or feature name at the bottom margin (or zero copy when context is unmistakable) so the viewer gets the 0.5-second "aha" moment.

## Asset Prerequisites & Transfer Limits (`asset_prerequisites` & `transfer_limits`)

- **Hard Transfer Guardrail (0.3-Second Baseline Recognition Rule)**: This move works **only if the audience already knows the intact baseline shape in 0.3 seconds**. Global icons can crush their own wordmarks; **small, emerging, or B2B brands must NEVER deform an unfamiliar logo** (passersby will assume a printing error). Instead, apply real physical deformation to a **universally recognized physical object** (a crushed can/carton, a sharply knotted/bent cable, a compressed protective shell, or a folded everyday container).
- **Brand & Scenario Limits**: Ensure the physical deformation aligns with the desired claim (never make durable hardware look fragile or broken); unsuitable for multi-spec e-commerce PDPs or urgent price-led promo banners.
