# Laws of UX Serial Position Effect & Peak-End Rule: Primacy-Recency Anchoring & Peak-End Storyboarding for Carousels and Long PDPs

[English Case Index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-lawsofux-serial-position-and-peak-end.md) · Cognitive Psychology & Multi-Slide Storyboarding

- **Symptom (`when_stuck`)**: A swipe carousel (Xiaohongshu / IG Slides 1→N), long e-commerce PDP, or mobile tab bar gives every slide equal visual weight—leaving readers unable to recall the core selling point and trailing off at the final screen with no clear conversion close.
- **Channels**: Social Swipe Carousels (Xiaohongshu / IG / TikTok Photo Mode), Long-Form PDP Storyboards, Mobile Navigation & Landing Pages.

## What the Source Material Shows (`observations`)

- **Serial Position Effect**:
  - Originating from **Hermann Ebbinghaus (1885)** and plotted as a U-shaped recall curve by **Bennet Murdock (1962)**: *"Users have a propensity to best remember the first and last items in a series."* Primacy (first items) and recency (last items) dominate memory while middle items are most readily forgotten.
  - Key design takeaways on `lawsofux.com`: Place the least important items in the middle of lists or navigation bars, and anchor the most important actions or messages at the **far left/right** or **first/last** positions.
- **Peak-End Rule**:
  - Grounded in **Daniel Kahneman, Barbara Fredrickson, Charles Schreiber, and Donald Redelmeier (1993)** (the cold-water immersion experiments): *"People judge an experience largely based on how they felt at its peak and at its end, rather than the total sum or average of every moment of the experience."*
  - Key design takeaways on `lawsofux.com`: Pay disproportionate attention to the most intense emotional/proof moment (the **Peak**) and the final wrap-up (the **End**), and eliminate negative frustration peaks first because negative spikes taint memory far more heavily than positive ones compensate.

## How Copy and Visuals Work Together (`copy_visual_synergy`)

- **First Slide / Hero (Primacy) sets the anchor**: Pair an unmistakable outcome visual with a razor-short hook headline so the primary value proposition locks into memory immediately.
- **Mid-Sequence Proof Peak (Peak) concentrates hard evidence**: Instead of spreading weak claims evenly across 6 slides, concentrate the strongest physical proof (real tear-down, side-by-side test result, dramatic before/after) into one unmistakable visual peak.
- **Final Slide / Bottom Section (Recency + End) delivers a frictionless close**: Pair a scannable 1-glance summary/selection checklist with a single low-friction CTA so the experience concludes with clarity and control.

## Reusable Visual Move (`reusable_move`)

1. **Enforce the "First–Peak–End" 3-Anchor Skeleton Across Multi-Slide & Long-Scroll Layouts**:
   - **Slide 1 (Primacy Anchor)**: Open directly with the sharpest contrast, pain point, or outcome visual—never waste Slide 1 on a vague brand intro or logo splash;
   - **Middle Slides (1 Dedicated Evidence Peak)**: Place supporting specs and context in the middle, but engineer **1 unmistakable proof peak** (destructive test, in-scale comparison, or real before/after teardown);
   - **Final Slide (Recency + Peak-End Close)**: End with a save-worthy cheat sheet or decision matrix + 1 clear CTA—never trail off with an empty "Thank You" or cluttered QR wall.
2. **Anchor Core Navigation at the Extremes**: Place highest-frequency actions at the far left and far right of mobile bottom bars and horizontal menus.
3. **Eliminate Frustration Peaks Before Polishing**: Fix blurry unreadable spec screenshots, broken links, or intrusive pop-ups before adding decorative polish elsewhere.

## Asset Prerequisites & Transfer Limits (`asset_prerequisites` & `transfer_limits`)

- **Asset Prerequisite**: Requires at least 1 verifiable piece of hard visual proof to carry the Peak slide (real test data, teardown photo, authentic before/after) plus a genuinely useful summary checklist for the End slide.
- **Transfer Limit**: Never fabricate or exaggerate test visuals to manufacture an artificial "Peak"; never bury the primary buying differentiator solely in the middle of a 7–9 slide deck without echoing it at the start and close.
