## 1. When Stuck (`when_stuck`)
- **Symptom**: Combining two fonts on a poster or landing page looks subtly "off" like a formatting mistake; setting a long headline in ALL CAPS eats up twice the horizontal width and forces a smaller point size; or a text-heavy specification/policy page looks like an uninviting, uniform wall of gray.

---

## 2. What the Source Material Shows (`observations`)
1. **ALL CAPS vs. Lowercase "Word Coastline" & Point-Size Experiment (Book p.182 & p.193 / PDF p.195, 206)**:
   - **Cognitive Mechanism (`giraffe` vs. `GIRAFFE`, `turtle` vs. `TURTLE`, p.193)**: Fluent readers recognize words not just letter-by-letter, but by the distinctive **word shape ("coastline")** formed by ascenders (`t, l, f, h`) and descenders (`g, p, y`). Setting words in `ALL CAPS` turns every single word into an identical, featureless **rectangular brick**, forcing slower letter-by-letter decoding.
   - **Point-Size Penalty (p.182)**: Within the exact same column width, an ALL-CAPS headline fits at only **20-point** type; switching to upper/lowercase (`Title Case` or `Sentence Case`) makes the word horizontally compact enough to set at **30-point** type—a **50% increase in point size** with far superior legibility.
2. **Same-Category Structure/Form Conflict vs. Cross-Category Contrast (Book p.195, 208, 219 / PDF p.208, 221, 232)**:
   - **Conflict Example 1 (`Work Hard / There is no shortcut`, p.195)**: Pairing a Script font (*Peoni Script*) on line 1 with an Oldstyle Italic serif (*Goudy Oldstyle Italic*) on line 2 fails because **both share a slanted, flowing form**—they conflict instead of contrasting. Replacing line 1 with an upright, heavy Slab Serif (*Aachen Bold*) creates instant contrast: upright & heavy vs. slanted & delicate.
   - **Conflict Example 2 (`FUNNY FARM / Health Insurance`, p.208 & p.219)**: Setting `FUNNY FARM` in small bold all-caps above `Health Insurance` in large light type creates **two competing focal points**—one shouts with weight/caps while the other shouts with size, leaving the reader unsure which is primary.
3. **Transforming a Gray Text Page via "Typographic Color" (`Ladle Rat Rotten Hut`, Book p.202–203 / PDF p.215–216)**:
   - **Before (p.202)**: Two separate stories set entirely in the same regular serif (*Warnock Pro Regular*) produce a flat, monotonous gray page where readers cannot even tell there are two distinct stories.
   - **After (p.203)**: Using zero colored ink—simply setting the main title in ultra-heavy *Aachen Bold* (creating a rich black anchor) and setting the second story in a light sans serif (*Eurostile*) with wider letter-spacing and leading (creating a light, airy texture)—instantly establishes tonal hierarchy and separates the two narratives.

## 3. How Copy and Visuals Work Together (`copy_visual_synergy`)
- **Make the reading roles clear**. Size, weight, and placement can each communicate priority. Check the actual reading order at the intended size.
- **Break up gray text walls with ultra-bold inline lead-ins (Book p.187 / PDF p.200)**: When FAQs, warranties, or technical notes lack room for illustrations, set the first `3–5` decisive words of each item in **Extra Bold / Black** so scanning buyers can grasp the full argument just by reading the bold anchors.

---

## 4. Reusable Visual Move (`reusable_move`)
1. **Assign type roles first**. One family can serve the whole layout. Two sans serif faces can also work when a condensed display face and a readable text face have distinct jobs.
2. **Use weight and width to clarify hierarchy**. Choose a difference readers can see at the target size. The examples in the book show specific settings for specific layouts.
3. **Combine scale, weight and form as needed**. Caps can work for short labels. Test longer headlines at their actual width and viewing size.

---

## 5. Asset Prerequisites & Transfer Limits (`asset_prerequisites` & `transfer_limits`)
- **Asset Prerequisite**: Choose type families with genuine drawn **Black/Heavy** weights and true **Italics**, avoiding computer-faked bolding or faux-slanted type (Book p.184, 194 / PDF p.197, 207).
- **Transfer Limit**: *Modern* serifs (like *Bodoni* or *Didot*, with extreme thick/thin stroke transitions) look striking at large display sizes (`36pt+`), but their hairline strokes vanish and create a "dazzling" effect in small body copy—use *Oldstyle* serifs or uniform *Sans Serifs* for body text (Book p.169 / PDF p.182).
