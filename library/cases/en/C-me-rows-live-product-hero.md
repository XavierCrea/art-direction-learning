# Rows Hero A/B Test & Haako Visual Rewrite: Replacing Dense Text Blocks with a Live Product Workspace or Operational Visual

[English Case Index](../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-me-rows-live-product-hero.md) · A/B Test Case Study & Before/After Landing Page Rewrites

- **Symptom (`when_stuck`)**: Your product has a clear workflow or unique physical mechanism, but the page uses a **huge block of text** or a generic static banner to explain how it works—so visitors bounce before reading past the second line.
- **Channels**: SaaS/tool landing pages, B2B/e-commerce hero sections with interactive configurators/selectors, product overview blocks.

## What the Source Material Shows (`observations`)

- **Case 1: Rows' Homepage Hero Test (`"Rows' homepage test that doubled conversions"`)**:
  - **Before**: A conventional SaaS hero layout (`Title + Subtitle + Sign-Up Button + Static Screenshot`).
  - **After**: Stripped the hero down to a crisp one-line headline and embedded a **live, interactive Rows spreadsheet workspace** directly above the fold—allowing visitors to click cells and experience the product immediately without creating an account.
  - **Result**: The Rows team reported that this hero test **doubled conversions**.
  - **Strict Transfer Conditions Noted by Harry Dry**: Putting the live workspace directly in the hero succeeded because Rows met two specific prerequisites:
    1. **Freemium model** (no paywall blocking instant use);
    2. **Spreadsheets are universally familiar** (zero learning curve to understand what to click).
- **Case 2: Haako & Banquist Landing Page Rewrites (`Harry Dry × Annie Maguire`)**:
  - **Haako Before**: Used an intimidating, dense paragraph of text (`huge text block`) trying to explain what the product was.
  - **Haako After**: *"Replaced huge text block with one sentence"* and *"Added an image to bring the product to life."*
  - **Banquist After**: Originally paired a genuinely unique culinary product experience with a generic headline that could apply to 50 other businesses; the rewrite followed the rule:
    > *"When you've got a unique product the golden rule is to let the product speak for itself."*

## How Copy and Visuals Work Together (`copy_visual_synergy`)

- **Copy subtracts (`1 sentence` positioning)**: Compresses 200 words of procedural explanation into a single outcome-driven headline.
- **Visuals / Interactive widgets add (`zero-friction self-proof`)**: Uses a 3-step visual diagram, live workspace preview, or interactive selector (e.g., clicking `4S–24S` string count and `50A–200A` current to highlight matching BMS models) so the visitor's eyes or mouse verify the value in under 5 seconds.

## Reusable Visual Move (`reusable_move`)

1. **Hunt Down and Replace "Explanatory Text Bricks"**: Whenever a page contains a text block longer than 4 lines trying to explain *what the product looks like* or *how it works step-by-step*, replace it with `1` punchy sentence + `1` annotated operational visual or step-by-step diagram.
2. **Deploy a Lightweight Interactive Selector When Familiar**: If your product selection follows parameters buyers already know by heart (e.g., battery chemistry `LiFePO4 / Li-ion`, series count `4S–32S`, continuous current `30A–300A`), embed a clean interactive filter card right in the first or second viewport instead of a static text wall.

## Asset Prerequisites & Transfer Limits (`asset_prerequisites` & `transfer_limits`)

- **Asset Prerequisite**: Any interactive widget or operational visual must be self-evident within 3 seconds without reading a manual.
- **Transfer Limit**: If a product's backend interface is complex, unfamiliar, or requires domain training (lacking the universal familiarity of a spreadsheet), **do not** dump the raw, unguided enterprise UI into the hero—it will overwhelm visitors. Use a simplified 3-step visual workflow diagram instead.
