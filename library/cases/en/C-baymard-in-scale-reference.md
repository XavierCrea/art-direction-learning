# Baymard In-Scale Reference Study: Eliminating Size Misjudgment for 42% of Buyers with Familiar Physical Anchors

[English Case Index](../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-baymard-in-scale-reference.md) · Independent Usability Testing & E-Commerce Benchmark Study

- **Symptom (`when_stuck`)**: A product page lists exact dimensions (`L × W × H`) or volume numbers in text, but the gallery only shows isolated white-background `"Cut Out"` photos—leaving buyers unable to gauge physical scale, mistaking a large unit for a compact one (or vice versa), and abandoning the purchase.
- **Channels**: E-commerce Product Detail Page (`PDP`) galleries, B2B industrial equipment catalogs, Amazon/Alibaba listing images, technical brochures.

## What the Source Material Shows (`observations`)

- Baymard Institute's large-scale usability testing reveals that **56%** of users’ very first action after arriving on a product page is to explore the product images (before reading the title, description, or scrolling), and **42%** of users actively try to establish a product's physical size and scale directly from its images (*"Users Try To Establish a Product’s Size from Its Images"*).
- Across Baymard's benchmark of the 60 top-grossing US and European e-commerce sites, **28%** still fail to provide even a single **"In Scale" image** for their best-selling products.
- **Negative vs. Positive Contrast (KitchenAid Pro vs. Classic Stand Mixer)**:
  - The KitchenAid Pro gallery only showed white-background cutout shots, causing test subjects to complain that they had *"no sense of scale"*.
  - The KitchenAid Classic gallery placed the mixer on a kitchen counter right next to several standard-sized **cupcakes**—allowing subjects to immediately deduce: *"I know how big cupcakes are, so this machine will fit in my kitchen."*
- **Wearables & Handhelds (Nordstrom vs. Patagonia Backpack / Sephora Hand Cream)**:
  - Nordstrom showed a North Face backpack only as a floating cutout, leaving buyers guessing how large it sat on a torso; Patagonia paired it with a human model wearing the pack.
  - Sephora displayed hand cream tubes on a white background labeled only `1 oz` vs. `5.4 oz`, forcing mental ounce-to-volume math, whereas showing a portable speaker held in a human palm instantly confirmed its pocketable scale.
- **Industrial & Large Goods (Home Depot Weber Grill / World Market vs. Sears Desk)**:
  - On Home Depot, a Weber grill had 5 images—all white-background cutouts. A test subject clicked through every thumbnail trying to gauge its footprint and ultimately abandoned the site in frustration.
  - On World Market, a wide-angle cutout photo made a desk look *"gigantic"*, whereas a Sears desk only 20% narrower looked appropriately sized because it was photographed inside a real room setting.

## How Copy and Visuals Work Together (`copy_visual_synergy`)

- **Visuals handle "0.5-second intuitive volume calibration"**: Placing the product alongside an everyday object of fixed, universally known dimensions (a human hand, a `175 cm` adult model, a standard cupcake/coffee mug, an A4 sheet, a standard `19-inch` server rack, or a laptop) provides instant spatial intuition without mental math.
- **Copy handles "exact tolerance verification"**: Pairing the in-scale photo with explicit dimensions (`5.4 oz / 160 ml` or `600 × 800 × 2000 mm`) prevents perspective distortion from misleading engineers or buyers with tight clearance constraints.
- **Video cannot replace a static In-Scale image**: Usability testing shows many shoppers never click play on product videos; at least one static `"In Scale"` image must live inside the primary thumbnail gallery.

## Reusable Visual Move (`reusable_move`)

1. Select a "Golden Scale Anchor" matched to how buyers physically interact with the product:
   - Handheld / desktop hardware $\rightarrow$ Held in one hand, next to a standard smartphone, A4 sheet, or coffee mug;
   - Wearable / carried gear $\rightarrow$ Worn by a human model with stated height;
   - Home / industrial equipment $\rightarrow$ Placed inside a standard operating environment (kitchen island, factory aisle, DIN rail, or next to a `19-inch` cabinet / technician).
2. Place this static `"In Scale"` image within the **first 3–4 thumbnails** of the primary PDP gallery rather than burying it at the bottom of the long description.
3. For catalogs with `10,000+` SKUs where individual lifestyle shoots are impractical, generate programmatic 1:1 silhouette comparison diagrams (similar to Amazon's human-silhouette scale overlays) as a baseline fallback.

## Asset Prerequisites & Transfer Limits (`asset_prerequisites` & `transfer_limits`)

- **Asset Prerequisite**: Verify the true physical dimensions of both the product and the reference object so perspective and proportions are 1:1 accurate.
- **Transfer Limit**: Never arbitrarily scale a product up or down when compositing or AI-generating background scenes (e.g., making a compact desktop speaker look like a floor-standing subwoofer, or a small BMS board look like a rack-mount inverter). Distorted scale references directly trigger returns and buyer disputes.
