# Baymard Descriptive Callouts Study: Bridging the "Scan Images, Skip Text" Gap with Leader Lines and Short Labels

[English Case Index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-baymard-descriptive-callouts.md) · Independent Usability Testing & E-Commerce Benchmark Study

- **Symptom (`when_stuck`)**: You shot high-resolution macro close-ups or wrote key selling points (waterproofing, pocket capacity, ventilation clearance) in the text description, but buyers swipe through the image gallery without reading the copy, stare at cropped close-ups in confusion (*"What the heck is this?"*), and bounce.
- **Channels**: E-commerce PDP image galleries, feature callout graphics, B2B industrial component detail diagrams.

## What the Source Material Shows (`observations`)

- Baymard Institute's research shows a fundamental tension: **56%** of users inspect product images immediately upon landing and strongly resist reading dense text blocks during initial screening, yet pure photographs cannot inherently convey non-visual attributes (such as waterproof ratings, material weight, or laptop compartment compatibility), and tightly cropped close-ups disorient viewers.
- Across top e-commerce sites, **52%** fail to include descriptive text or graphics on any of their product images.
- **Anti-Pattern 1 (Lowe's Grill Burner Close-Up)**: A gallery image showed a tightly cropped metal tube with flames and zero text annotations. A test subject reacted in frustration: *"What the heck is this? I see flames, I have no idea what it's for."*
- **Anti-Pattern 2 (Missed Weatherproof Attribute on Patio Cushions)**: A shopper wanted to know if outdoor sofa cushions could stay outside in the rain. After flipping through all product images and finding no waterproof indicator, they skimmed the page, missed the phrase `"all weather fabric"` buried inside a paragraph, and abandoned the product.
- **Positive Benchmarks (Waterproof Speaker / Lowe's Fridge Dimensions / Amazon Laptop Bag)**:
  - Adding splashing water graphics paired with a bold `"100% Waterproof"` callout on a Bluetooth speaker;
  - Drawing dimension arrows and required air-clearance callouts directly onto a Lowe's refrigerator photo (information otherwise buried inside a PDF manual);
  - Pulling leader lines from a laptop bag's compartments labeled `"Fits up to 15.6\" laptop"` and `"Water resistant"` on Amazon—all caused fast-swiping test subjects to pause, grasp the core differentiators, and explore the product in depth.
- **Mobile Font-Size Failure (HP Mobile Hero Banner)**: HP reused a desktop banner with baked-in text on mobile viewports; apart from the main headline `"Save up to $350"`, the orange CTA button and two lines of supporting copy shrank into an illegible blur.

## How Copy and Visuals Work Together (`copy_visual_synergy`)

- **Photos and visual graphics lock in "physical location and sensory state"**: Showing where a component sits on the assembly, its surface texture, or visual cues like water splashes.
- **Leader lines and short callout labels (`2–6 words`) deliver "instant qualitative and quantitative clarity"**: Pointing directly to the relevant feature (`"304 Stainless Steel Burner"`, `"IPX7 Waterproof"`, `"Fits 15.6\" Laptop"`) saves buyers from guessing what a macro crop shows or hunting through paragraphs of prose.

## Reusable Visual Move (`reusable_move`)

1. Identify the top `2–4` non-obvious selling points (waterproof/fireproof rating, core alloy/material, compatible dimensions, installation clearance) or ambiguous close-up crops that buyers care about most.
2. Overlay clean leader lines pointing to the exact physical part, paired with high-contrast short labels (`3–6` English words or `≤10` Chinese characters per callout).
3. For extreme macro close-ups, include a small inset thumbnail showing where the part sits on the full unit, or add a callout naming both the part and its buyer benefit.
4. **Enforce the Mobile Legibility Guard**: Any text baked into an image must remain effortlessly readable when scaled down to a `375px`-wide mobile screen; move multi-sentence explanations into HTML captions below the image.

## Asset Prerequisites & Transfer Limits (`asset_prerequisites` & `transfer_limits`)

- **Asset Prerequisite**: Leader lines must point to the exact engineering component, and every stated metric (IP rating, load capacity, clearance dimension) must be backed by verified specifications.
- **Transfer Limit**: Cap callouts at `≤4` per image—never paste entire specification paragraphs onto a photo; never serve desktop banners with tiny baked-in text directly to mobile viewports.
